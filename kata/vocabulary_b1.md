## Concepts

### 1. Word families by topic
Vocabulary at B1 is organised by topic groups: work, travel, health, money, emotions.
Key principle: learn words in context, not as isolated lists.

### 2. Word formation
Recognise and produce different forms of the same root using suffixes:
- `-tion / -ment / -ness` → noun
- `-ful / -less / -ive / -ed / -ing` → adjective
- `-ly` → adverb

Example: succeed → success (n) → successful (adj) → successfully (adv)

### 3. Collocations (Fixed expressions)
Fixed verb+noun pairs with no internal logic — must be memorised:
- make a decision / mistake
- do homework / an exam
- take a risk / a photo
- have a shower
- pay attention

### 4. Near-synonyms
Words with similar translation but different usage rules:
- say vs tell: say + words / tell + person
- look vs watch vs see: intentional static / process over time / passive perception
- bring vs take: toward / away from speaker
- lend vs borrow: give on loan / receive on loan

### 5. Linking words (Discourse markers)
Used to build coherent speech and writing:
- Addition: also, moreover, in addition
- Contrast: however, although, despite
- Cause: because, since, due to
- Result: therefore, so, consequently

Key distinction:
- *although* + full clause (subject + verb)
- *despite* + noun / noun phrase / gerund
